# India Urban Flooding & Waterlogging Report (2024-2026)

## Overview
Research confirms that rain-induced waterlogging and flooding—specifically cases where roads are blocked and water enters homes—is a **pan-India problem**. As of 2024-2026, this issue has been documented in **all 28 states** and **all 8 Union Territories** of India.

## Regional Breakdown & Patterns

### 1. Urban Crisis (Drainage Failure)
*   **Characteristics:** Chronic failure of storm water drains (SWD) in metropolitan hubs.
*   **Key Locations:** Maharashtra (Mumbai, Pune), Karnataka (Bengaluru), Tamil Nadu (Chennai), Kerala (Kochi), Telangana (Hyderabad), Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal (Kolkata), Gujarat, and Goa.
*   **Note:** Goa reports some of the highest annual rainfall, contributing to severe urban inundation.

### 2. River & Plain States (Overflow/Stagnation)
*   **Characteristics:** Flat topography leading to prolonged water stagnation and riverine flooding.
*   **Key Locations:** Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Assam (Brahmaputra valley), Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, and Madhya Pradesh.

### 3. Mountain & Hill States (Flash Floods)
*   **Characteristics:** High-velocity torrents, cloudbursts, and landslides that destroy roads and flood valley-bottom homes.
*   **Key Locations:** Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, and Meghalaya.

### 4. Dry & Arid States (Sudden Inundation)
*   **Characteristics:** Sudden heavy rainfall on hard/sandy soil with poor absorption rates.
*   **Key Locations:** Rajasthan (Jaipur, Jodhpur), Haryana, and Punjab.

## Union Territories (UTs)
All 8 UTs are affected, including:
- **Delhi (NCR):** Severe urban hotspots.
- **J&K & Ladakh:** Urban floods and cloudbursts.
- **Chandigarh:** Sector-specific waterlogging.
- **Puducherry, A&N, Lakshadweep, DNH&DD:** Coastal and tropical rain flooding.
