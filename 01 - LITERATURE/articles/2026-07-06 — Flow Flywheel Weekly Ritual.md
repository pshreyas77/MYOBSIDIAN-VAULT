## For future Claude
This is a comprehensive article detailing a productivity framework called the "Flow Flywheel." It explains goal displacement, introduces a 7-layer Goal Stack, and provides a 3-step protocol for weekly and daily planning to ensure actions align with long-term objectives. It is a high-level strategic guide.

## Core Concept: The Flow Flywheel
A 90-minute weekly ritual to prevent [[goal displacement]] and align daily actions with long-term objectives by leveraging the neuroscience of [[flow state]].

## Key Ideas
- **Goal Displacement (Robert Michels):** When the process of achieving a goal becomes the goal itself. Illustrated by the story of Marcus, a PhD student who was "being a PhD student" rather than pursuing a PhD.
- **The Goal Stack:** A 7-layer hierarchy connecting daily actions to long-term purpose:
  1. Purpose
  2. High Hard Goals (1-5 years)
  3. Annual Goals
  4. Quarterly Goals
  5. Monthly Goals
  6. Weekly Goals
  7. Dominoes (daily high-leverage tasks)
- **Dominoes:** High-leverage actions that, if completed, move you closer to your goal more than anything else. Based on the 80/20 rule (closer to 5/95). Limit to 3 per week, broken into 3 specific tasks per day.
- **Causal Chains:** Thinking in terms of cause and effect to identify the single action that will most directly lead to the desired outcome.

## The 3-Step Protocol
1. **Review Goals (15 min):** Assess the past week against the Goal Stack. Answer: How did my actions drive me toward my goals? What is the gap? What specific actions this week close that gap?
2. **Identify Weekly Dominoes (30 min):** For each goal, ask: "What single action would move this forward most dramatically?" Limit to 3 dominoes per week.
3. **Daily Flow Flywheel (15 min):** At the end of each workday, review the Goal Stack, identify tomorrow's critical dominoes, and schedule them on the calendar. Break each domino into wildly specific micro-goals.

## Neuroscientific Basis
- The [[dorsolateral prefrontal cortex]] (executive command center) downregulates when given a clear goal.
- This hands control to faster, more intuitive networks that drive flow.
- Vague goals keep the prefrontal cortex hyperactive, causing internal noise that blocks flow.
- Flow is indiscriminate; it feels like progress regardless of whether it is. The Flywheel ensures flow is allocated correctly.

## Quotes
