## Purpose and Background

### The Problem: Brahmin Dominance in Madras Presidency (1915)
- Brahmin population: **3.1%** of total
- Yet held **60–79%** of government jobs and **~75%** of positions in higher education and professional fields
- Indian National Congress in Madras Presidency dominated by Brahmin leadership
- Non-Brahmin communities (Mudaliars, Naidus, Reddys, Kammas, Kapus, Gounders, Vellalas, etc.) systematically excluded despite numeric majority

### The Response
The **Non-Brahmin Manifesto** (authored by Theagaraya Chetty, 1916) officially launched the non-Brahmin movement — demanding proportional political representation and an end to Brahmin monopoly over government employment and education.

---

## Governance (1920–1937)

### Electoral Success
The Justice Party won Madras Presidency elections under Dyarchy and held power for **13 of the 17 years between 1920 and 1937**, forming ministries in:
- **1920–1926** (first ministry)
- **1932–1937** (second ministry)
- Interrupted by Congress ministries when Congress won some seats

### Key Policies Enacted
| Policy | Year | Impact |
|--------|------|--------|
| **Communal G.O. 3136** | 1921 | Reserved government posts for non-Brahmins — India's first statutory caste-based reservation |
| Educational expansion | 1920s | More non-Brahmin access to Madras University, professional education (medicine, engineering, law) |
| Self-help schools & hostels | 1920s | For backward class students facing caste discrimination in education |
| Tenant protections | 1920s | Early land reform protecting farmers against Brahmin landowners |

### The Communal G.O. — Legacy
The 1921/1922 Government Orders establishing **India's first statutory caste-based reservation system** for government appointments. Faced legal challenges; Supreme Court later struck it down — but the precedent was set and it directly influenced post-independence reservation policy under Ambedkar's guidance.

## Decline and Periyar's Takeover

### The 1937 Defeat
Justice Party suffered a **crushing electoral defeat** to Congress in 1937. This defeat radicalized the party and opened the door for Periyar.

### Periyar's Role
- Joined Justice Party in **1935**; elected President **1938/1939** (while in prison for anti-Hindi agitations)
- Transformed the party from an elite landowning gentry-caste association into a **radical mass movement**
- Shifted ideology: from caste-representation politics → rationalist anti-Hinduism, women's rights, Dravidian identity

---

## Transformation: Justice Party → Dravidar Kazhagam (1944)

At the **Salem Conference (August 27, 1944)**, Periyar moved the resolution to rename and transform the Justice Party into **Dravidar Kazhagam (DK)** — permanently withdrawing from electoral politics to focus purely on social reform and caste eradication.

**Why the name change?**
- "Justice Party" had become associated with elite caste-association politics of landed gentry
- "Dravidar Kazhagam" signaled: mass movement, Dravidian identity, revolutionary social transformation

## Open Questions (from original synthesis) — **ANSWERED 2026-07-18**

### 1. Communal G.O. 3136 Implementation & 1937 Transition
**Answered:** The 1921/1922 G.O.s were expanded by **G.O. No. 1129 (Dec 15, 1928)** guaranteeing proportionate representation for Brahmins, non-Brahmins, Christians, Muslims, and "depressed classes." The reservation framework **remained operational** under the 1937 Congress ministry (Rajaji) and early Republic. The lineage was broken by the Supreme Court in **1951** in *State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan* — struck down as violating Article 16(2). This led to the **First Constitutional Amendment** (1951) to protect social justice measures.

### 2. Periyar's 1932 Soviet Visit & Rationalism
**Answered:** Three-month stay in USSR was a turning point — shifted from purely rationalist anti-religion to **socio-economic structuralism**. Upon return, Periyar adopted a **Marxist stance on socio-economic issues** (not total abolition of private ownership). Collaborated with **M. Singaravelar** to synthesize socialist goals with Self-Respect ideals — marking the **politicization of the Self-Respect Movement** with Marxist analysis of caste.

### 3. Periyar vs. Phule: Independent Development or Inheritance?
**Answered:** **Independent development.** Periyar's radicalization was driven by local events: the **Gurukulam controversy** (segregated Brahmin/non-Brahmin dining) and the **1925 rejection of his communal representation resolutions** in Congress. While Phule is recognized as a pioneer in the same anti-caste tradition, Periyar independently engineered a radical mass movement in the South.

### 4. Dravidian Identity vs. Caste Identity — Genuine Shift or Tactical?
**Answered:** **Both, sequentially.**
- **Phase 1 (Periyar/DK):** Genuine broad cultural/political front against "Aryan-Brahmin" influence — total social revolution, caste annihilation.
- **Phase 2 (DMK institutionalization):** Captured by **dominant non-Brahmin landlord castes** (Vellalars, Mudaliars, Naidus). Shifted to **"graded inequality"** — anti-Brahmin rhetoric maintained, but hierarchical privilege and caste-based patronage reproduced by newly dominant non-Brahmin elites. Dalits and marginalized groups remained underrepresented in power structures.

## Legacy

- **Justice Party (1916–1944)** = first non-Congress political organization in South India; pioneered India's reservation system
- **Dravidar Kazhagam (1944–)** = transformed party; non-electoral social reform under Periyar
- **DMK split (1949)** = Annadurai broke away to contest elections; won Madras State in 1967
- **AIADMK** = later DMK offshoot; continues Dravidian political tradition
- **Reservation policy** = Justice Party's Communal G.O. is the direct ancestor of Article 16(4) and central legislature reservation

## Relationship with Ambedkar

| Periyar / Justice Party | Ambedkar |
|------------------------|----------|
| Tamil Nadu focus | National (Maharashtra + broader) |
| Cultural Dravidian identity | Dalit identity across castes |
| Self-Reform (individual dignity) | Annihilation of caste (structural) |
| No religion → preferred Buddhism eventually | Left Hinduism → converted to Buddhism (1956) |
| Initially accepted varna, opposed Brahmin dominance | Rejected varna itself as a system |
| Ambedkar supported Periyar's communal representation demand | — |

Notable: Ambedkar invited Periyar to speak in Tamil Nadu; both saw Brahminical Hinduism as the core problem.
