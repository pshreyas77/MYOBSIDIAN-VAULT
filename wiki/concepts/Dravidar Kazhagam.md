---

## Formation

### Why the Name Change?
Periyar renamed the Justice Party to Dravidar Kazhagam because:
1. **"Justice Party"** had become associated with elite caste-association politics of landed gentry and professionals — fighting for their own share, not revolutionary change
2. **"Dravidar Kazhagam"** (Dravidian Association) signaled: mass movement, Dravidian identity, social transformation over political power

### The Salem Conference (1944)
At the Salem Conference, Periyar successfully moved a resolution to:
- Rename the party to **Dravidar Kazhagam**
- Permanently withdraw from electoral politics
- Focus purely on **social reform and caste eradication**
- Advocate for a sovereign **Dravida Nadu** (separate South Indian nation)

## Ideology

### Core Pillars
| Pillar | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Atheism / Rationalism** | Hinduism itself — not just Brahmin dominance — is the problem. Rejected varnashrama dharma entirely |
| **Anti-superstition** | Public rationalist critique; burns of sacred threads, Hindu texts |
| **Caste annihilation** | Absolute opposition to caste hierarchy — not reform but eradication |
| **Radical feminism** | Women's rights: self-respect marriages, property rights, divorce, birth control, widow remarriage |
| **Dravidian identity** | Anti-Hindi imposition; promotion of Tamil; Dravidian federalism against centralized state |
| **Separation of religion from state** | Religion is personal; politics must be secular |

### Contrast: Before vs. After Periyar
| Dimension | Justice Party (pre-1938) | Dravidar Kazhagam (post-1944) |
|-----------|-------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Membership | Elite landed gentry, professionals | Mass movement |
| Goal | Political share for non-Brahmins | Social revolution |
| Hinduism | Anti-Brahmin, within Hinduism | Hinduism itself is the problem |
| Women | Moderate reforms | Radical: self-respect marriage, rights |
| Elections | Contested (governed Madras Presidency) | Refused — pure reform movement |
| Language | Not central | Anti-Hindi; pro-Tamil |

## The 1949 Split

### M. R. Radhakrishnan and C. N. Annadurai wanted to:
- Contest elections under the DK banner
- Return to parliamentary politics

### Periyar's position:
- Electoral politics would dilute the radical social reform mission
- Contesting elections = betraying the movement's purpose

### The Break
Annadurai led a split from DK in **1949** to form the **Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK)** — a political party committed to electoral participation. Periyar opposed this split. He campaigned **against** the DMK in the 1957 and 1962 elections.

### DMK's Success
DMK won Madras State in **1967** — beginning decades of Dravidian party dominance in Tamil Nadu politics.

## Key Practices of DK (Periyar Era)

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Self-Respect Marriage (Suyamariyathai)** | Ceremonies without Brahmin pandits; inter-caste; equal rights for women |
| **Temple Entry Advocacy** | Demanding Dalit access to temples |
| **Rationalist Critique** | Public burns of sacred threads, Hindu texts, anti-superstition demonstrations |
| **Widow Remarriage** | Active promotion and self-respect ceremonies for widows |
| **Birth Control Advocacy** | Early family planning and women's autonomy |
| **Dravidian Home** | Hostels for non-Brahmin students facing caste discrimination in education (inherited from C. Natesa Mudaliar's earlier work) |

## Dravida Nadu Demand

DK formally advocated for a **separate Dravida Nadu** — a sovereign South Indian nation:
- Against centralized Hindi-imposing state
- For Dravidian linguistic and cultural self-determination
- The demand faded as Dravidian parties won power within the Indian constitutional framework

---

## Legacy

- **Justice Party → Dravidar Kazhagam → DMK** = the lineage of Dravidian politics in Tamil Nadu
- **Pure social reform model** = Periyar's DK vs. Annadurai's DMK = ongoing debate about whether caste liberation needs electoral power or only social revolution
- **Contemporary resonance**: TVK (Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam) — Vijay's party — cites Periyar in rhetoric but has neither mass movement nor social reform practice. Populist recombination of Periyar's symbol without his method.
