# Atisha

## Overview
**Dates**: 982–1054 CE  
**Origin**: Bengal (Vikramashila monastery)  
**Significance**: 11th c. Bengali monk; fled to Tibet; symbol of eastern Indian Buddhism's terminal phase

## Core Role
- **Vikramashila abbot** during Buddhism's final flourishing in eastern India
- **Invited to Tibet** (1042) by King Yeshe-Ö to restore pure Buddhism
- **Founded Kadam school** — precursor to Gelug (Dalai Lama's lineage)
- **Wrote Bodhipathapradīpa** (Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment) — lamrim (gradual path) template

## Historical Significance for Decline

| Aspect | Significance |
|--------|--------------|
| **Terminal figure** | Last major Indian Buddhist master to go to Tibet |
| **Brain drain symbol** | India's best Buddhist minds leaving for Tibet = institutional collapse |
| **Vikramashila's end** | Destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khalji ~1203, ~150 years after Atisha's departure |
| **Tibetan preservation** | Atisha's curriculum preserves what India lost |

## Key Works
- **Bodhipathapradīpa** (Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment)
- **Caryāgītikośa** (Treasury of Practice Songs)
- **Satyaṃcakrāṅgikā** (Wheel of Truth)
