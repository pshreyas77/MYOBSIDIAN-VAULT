# Dharmakirti

## Overview
**Dates**: ~600–660 CE  
**School**: Buddhist logic/epistemology (Pramāṇavāda)  
**Significance**: Last great Buddhist philosopher of "classical" period; Pramāṇavārttika is foundational for Buddhist logic

## Core Philosophy

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Pramāṇa Theory** | Two valid means of knowledge: perception (pratyakṣa) and inference (anumāna) |
| **Apoha (Exclusion)** | Universals are mental constructions; words refer by excluding non-referents |
| **Momentariness (Kṣaṇikatva)** | All entities are momentary; causality = necessary succession |
| **Self-Refuting Critique** | Refutes Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta using their own logical standards |

## Key Works
- **Pramāṇavārttika** (Commentary on Dignāga's Pramāṇasamuccaya) — magnum opus
- **Pramāṇaviniścaya** (Determination of Valid Cognition)
- **Hetubindu** (Drop of Reason) — syllogistic logic
- **Santānāntarasiddhi** (Proof of Other Minds)

## Historical Role in Buddhism's Decline

| Aspect | Significance |
|--------|--------------|
| **Terminal brilliance** | Peak of Buddhist logic; no major Indian Buddhist philosophers after him |
| **Tibetan transmission** | Entire Tibetan Buddhist curriculum (Gelug, Sakya, Kagyu) built on Dharmakirti |
| **Hindu absorption** | Navya-Nyāya (Gangesha, 12th c.) directly engages and absorbs Dharmakirtian logic |
| **No Indian successors** | After Dharmakirti, Buddhist philosophy flourishes in Tibet, not India |
