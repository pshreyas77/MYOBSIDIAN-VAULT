# Kabir

## Overview
**Dates**: 1398–1448 CE  
**Caste**: Julaha (weaver) — Muslim birth, Hindu upbringing  
**Identity**: Contested and politically appropriated by all sides  
**Tradition**: Nirguṇa Bhakti (formless divine)  

## Critical Correction
**Kabir was NOT Dalit.** He was OBC (weaver). His community was relatively prosperous with some social mobility. Recent scholarship (Hawley, Vaudeville, Lorenzen) notes his anti-caste rhetoric was more anti-Brahminical than pro-Dalit.

## Core Philosophy

| Tenet | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Nirguṇa Bhakti** | Formless divine (nirguṇa Brahman) accessible without priestly mediation |
| **Anti-ritualism** | Rejected idol worship, pilgrimage, fasting, sacred thread, Quran, Vedas |
| **Anti-caste** | *"Jaṭi-pāṭī pūchhe nahiṅ koī, hari ko bhaje so hari kā hoī"* (No one asks caste; whoever worships Hari belongs to Hari) |
| **Syncretic idiom** | Used both Hindu (Rama, Hari) and Muslim (Allah, Khuda, Pir) names for divine |

## Political Appropriation

| Group | Claim | Reality |
|-------|-------|---------|

| Dalit movements | Anti-caste icon | He was OBC, not Dalit |
| Secularists | "Synthesis" | Ignores radical rejection of both traditions |
