# Nagarjuna

## Overview
**Dates**: ~150–250 CE  
**School**: Madhyamaka (Middle Way)  
**Significance**: Founder of Madhyamaka; philosophical bridge between early Buddhism and Mahayana; "second Buddha" in Tibetan tradition

## Core Philosophy

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Śūnyatā (Emptiness)** | All phenomena lack inherent existence (svabhāva); dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) = emptiness |
| **Two Truths** | Conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) vs ultimate truth (paramārtha-satya) |
| **Refutation of Essence** | Mūlamadhyamakakārikā systematically refutes all metaphysical positions (existence, non-existence, both, neither) |
| **Dialectical Method** | Reductio ad absurdum (prasaṅga) — shows contradictions in opponent views without asserting own thesis |

## Key Works
- **Mūlamadhyamakakārikā** (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way) — primary text
- **Vigrahavyāvartanī** (The Dispeller of Disputes) — defends emptiness against critics
- **Śūnyatāsaptati** (Seventy Verses on Emptiness)
- **Yuktiṣaṣṭikā** (Sixty Verses on Reasoning)
- **Ratnāvalī** (Precious Garland) — advice to king

## Historical Role in Buddhism's Decline

| Aspect | Significance |
|--------|--------------|
| **Philosophical peak** | Represents height of Buddhist philosophical sophistication |
| **Mahayana systematization** | Provided philosophical foundation for Mahayana expansion |
| **Absorption vector** | Śaṅkara's Advaita Vedānta directly engages and absorbs Madhyamaka arguments |
| **Tantric precursor** | Emptiness philosophy enables Vajrayāna development |
