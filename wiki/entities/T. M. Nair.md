## Role in Justice Party

### Co-Founder and Ideological Leader
- **Named the party** — he coined the name "Justice Party," symbolizing the demand for social justice and political representation for non-Brahmin communities
- First honorary editor of the party's English newspaper, ***Justice***
- Alongside P. Theagaraya Chetty and C. Natesa Mudaliar, formed the founding troika

### The Feud with Theagaraya Chetty
T. M. Nair and P. Theagaraya Chetty had a significant personal/organizational rivalry that threatened to split the party before it fully formed. **C. Natesa Mudaliar** reconciled them in 1916, enabling the party's successful formation. This makes Natesa Mudaliar's role as unifier historically critical.

---

## London Mission (1918–1919)

### The Mission
T. M. Nair led a delegation to **London to petition the British Parliament** for non-Brahmin communal representation before the passage of the India Act reforms. This was a constitutional advocacy strategy — appealing to the British to recognize non-Brahmin communities as a distinct political constituency.

### Death in London
Nair **died in London during this mission in 1919**, at age 51. He did not live to see the Government of India Act 1919 or the party's electoral success in 1920.
