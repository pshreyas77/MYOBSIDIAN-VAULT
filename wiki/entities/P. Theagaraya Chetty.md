## Role in Justice Party

### First President (1917–1925)
Theagaraya Chetty served as **first President of the Justice Party** from 1917 until his death in 1925. He was the organizational builder — raised funds, built party structure, and provided business credibility to the movement.

### The Non-Brahmin Manifesto (1916)
Authored the **"Non-Brahmin Manifesto"** in 1916 — the foundational document that officially launched the non-Brahmin movement in the Madras Presidency. This manifesto articulated the demand for proportional political representation and an end to Brahmin monopoly over government employment and education.

### Relationship with T. M. Nair
Theagaraya Chetty and T. M. Nair had significant personal and organizational rivalry that threatened to derail the party formation. C. Natesa Mudaliar reconciled them. After Nair's death in London (1919), Theagaraya Chetty became the dominant organizational figure until his own death in 1925.

## Major Social Initiative: Midday Meal Scheme

### First School Midday Meal in Madras
Theagaraya Chetty **instrumental in starting the first school midday meal scheme** in Madras Presidency. This was a pioneering social welfare initiative designed to:
- Improve student retention among poor and marginalized communities
- Address caste discrimination that kept non-Brahmin children out of schools
- Build educational equity from the ground up

This prefigures the later national midday meal scheme by nearly a century.

## Legacy

- **Died 1925** — before Periyar's radical transformation of the party
- His Justice Party was primarily a **political power-sharing movement** for non-Brahmin elites
- The **radical social revolution** came only with Periyar's takeover (1938–1944)
- The midday meal scheme remains one of the earliest and most concrete social welfare innovations credited to the non-Brahmin movement
