## Political Trajectory

### Justice Party & DK (1930s–1949)
- Joined **Justice Party** in the 1930s; became a protege of **Periyar E. V. Ramasamy**
- When Periyar transformed the Justice Party into **Dravidar Kazhagam (DK)** in **1944**, Annadurai became its **General Secretary**
- Served as Periyar's lieutenant; edited DK's publications *Kudiyarasu* and *Viduthalai*

### The 1949 Split: DK → DMK
| Issue | Periyar (DK) | Annadurai (DMK) |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| **Electoral politics** | Refused — pure social reform | Demanded electoral entry |
| **Post-independence strategy** | Separatist (Dravida Nadu) | Federalist (autonomy within India) |
| **Leadership style** | Charismatic, anti-institutional | Organizational, party-building |
| **Language** | Tamil purity; anti-Hindi | Pragmatic Tamil nationalism |

**Annadurai's rationale**: Social revolution requires political power; DK's refusal to contest elections left the field to Congress. He formed **Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK)** on **17 September 1949** at a meeting in Madurai. Periyar opposed the split and campaigned against DMK in 1957 and 1962 elections.

---

## DMK Founding & Early Struggles (1949–1967)

### Organizational Innovation
- Built DMK as a **cadre-based mass party** — not a personal vehicle
- Used **Tamil cinema** as political medium (scripts by Annadurai, Karunanidhi, MGR)
- Created parallel structures: **Dravidar Kazhagam (women's wing)**, **DMK student wing**, **labour wing**

### Key Electoral Milestones
| Year | Result | Significance |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 1952 | 15 seats (Madras Legislative Assembly) | First electoral test; proved DK's abstention was a tactical error |
| 1957 | 15 seats; Annadurai elected to Lok Sabha | Entered national parliament |
| 1962 | 50 seats; Annadurai lost Lok Sabha but won Assembly by-election | Consolidation |
| 1967 | **138/234 seats** — landslide | First non-Congress government in a major state; Annadurai becomes CM |

## Chief Ministership (1967–1969)

### Policy Achievements (20 months in office)
| Policy | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Legalized Self-Respect Marriages** | 1967 Hindu Marriage (Madras Amendment) Act — validated Suyamariyathai ceremonies |
| **Two-language formula** | Rejected three-language formula; Tamil + English only in Tamil Nadu |
| **Subsidized rice** | "Anna's rice" — 1 measure rice for ₹1; foundation of Tamil Nadu's PDS |
| **Renamed Madras State → Tamil Nadu** | 1968 legislative resolution; implemented 1969 |
| **Waived agricultural loans** | For small farmers; pro-rural base consolidation |
| **Government jobs for graduates** | Employment guarantee scheme for educated unemployed |

### Style of Governance
- **"Anna's rule"** — emphasis on integrity, austerity, Tamil pride
- First CM to **lie in state at Rajaji Hall** (1969) — massive public mourning
- Died in office **3 February 1969**; succeeded by V. R. Nedunchezhiyan (interim), then M. Karunanidhi

## Intellectual Contributions

### Oratory & Writing
- **Master of Tamil oratory** — "Anna's Tamil" combined classical purity with colloquial accessibility
- Wrote extensively in *Dravida Nadu*, *Kudi Arasu*, *Periyar* journals
- Key works: *Arya Mayai* (Aryan Illusion), *Thamizhachi*, *Sathiyam* (speeches)

### Ideological Positioning
| Dimension | Annadurai's Stance |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Dravida Nadu** | Demanded initially; dropped after 1963 constitutional amendment (anti-secession law) — pragmatic shift to state autonomy |
| **Anti-Hindi** | Led 1965 anti-Hindi agitation; "Hindi imperialism" rhetoric but within constitutional framework |
| **Caste** | Anti-Brahmin, pro-backward-class; but **pragmatic coalition-builder** — included Vanniyars, Thevars, Dalits |
| **Religion** | Rationalist (Periyar-influenced); but **tactically accommodated** popular Hindu sentiment — "Tamil kadavul" (Tamil god) concept |
| **Federalism** | Strong state autonomy; Rajamannar Committee (Centre-State relations) |

## Relationship with Periyar

| Phase | Dynamic |
|-------|---------|
| **1930s–1944** | Disciple → close aide; managed Periyar's tours, publications |
| **1944–1949** | DK General Secretary; heir apparent |
| **1949–1969** | Rival; Periyar called DMK "betrayal" and campaigned against it |
| **Post-1969** | Periyar attended Annadurai's funeral; later acknowledged DMK's electoral success validated Annadurai's strategy |

### The Core Disagreement
Periyar: **Social revolution precedes political power** — electoral politics corrupts reform.
Annadurai: **Political power enables social reform** — without state power, reform is symbolic.

History validated both: DMK institutionalized Dravidian politics; DK preserved radical critique.

## Succession & Legacy

### Immediate Succession (1969)
| Contender | Outcome |
|-----------|---------|
| **M. Karunanidhi** | Won party leadership; CM 1969–1971, 1971–1976, 1989–1991, 1996–2001 |
| **M. G. Ramachandran (MGR)** | Expelled 1972 → founded AIADMK (1972); CM 1977–1987 |

### Enduring Legacy
1. **First non-Congress CM in a major state** — broke Congress hegemony (1967)
2. **Institutionalized Dravidian politics** — DMK became permanent alternative to Congress/BJP in Tamil Nadu
3. **Tamil Nadu's welfare model** — subsidized rice, midday meals, free education roots trace to Annadurai
4. **Federalism champion** — Rajamannar Committee; template for state autonomy demands
5. **Language politics** — Two-language formula remains Tamil Nadu's policy
