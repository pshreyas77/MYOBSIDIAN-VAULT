# Charvaka / Lokayata — Primary Source Deep Dive

**For future Claude:** This addresses Philosophy MOC Gap #1. The Bṛhaspati Sūtra is lost. No complete original text survives. Our knowledge comes from opponent commentaries and one complete skeptical text (Jayarāśi). Correct the common framing: Cārvākas did not uniformly "reject inference" — this is an opponent caricature. Distinguish materialist from skeptical variants.

---

## 1. The Source Problem

The foundational text **Bṛhaspati Sūtra** is lost. What survives:

| Source | Date | Type | Reliability | Key Content |
|--------|------|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa: Tattvopaplavasiṃha** | 8th c. CE | Primary (skeptical variant) | **High** | Radical skepticism; questions all pramāṇas including perception |
| **Madhavacharya: Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha Ch. 1** | 14th c. CE | Opponent's summary | **Low** | Hostile; presents hedonist caricature |
| **Śāntarakṣita: Tattvasaṅgraha Ch. 22** | 8th c. CE | Buddhist refutation | **Medium** | Accurate on opponent views |
| **Kumārila Bha Bhaṭṭa: Ślokavārttika** | 8th c. CE | Mīmāṃsā refutation | **Medium** | Accurate but polemical |
| **Manusmṛti XII.118** | ~2nd c. BCE | Legal condemnation | **Low** | Polemical; "nāstika" as legal category |
| **Mahābhārata Śānti Parva** | ~4th c. CE | Epic interpolation | **Medium** | Jābāli episode; materialist arguments |
| **Maṇimēkalai (Tamil)** | ~6th c. CE | Tamil epic | **Medium** | References pre-Cārvāka materialism (5 elements) |

## 2. Epistemology: The "Inference" Debate

**Correction to common framing**: The claim that Cārvākas "rejected inference entirely" comes from opponents. Current scholarship (Bhattacharya, Franco, Preisendanz) distinguishes:

| Position | Description | Attribution |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Perception-only (pratyakṣa-mātra)** | Some Cārvākas rejected inference for *metaphysical claims* (afterlife, god, soul) because such inferences are not grounded in verifiable perception | Likely mainstream Cārvāka |
| **Inference for mundane matters** | Other Cārvākas likely accepted inference for everyday causation (fire from smoke) but rejected scriptural authority (śabda) as a pramāṇa | Plausible variant |
| **Jayarāśi's radical skepticism** | Questions even the validity of perception itself — a skeptical, not materialist, position | Jayarāśi specifically |

**Key insight**: Jayarāśi is a *skeptic* (tattvopaplavasiṃha = "upsetting of principles"), not a straightforward materialist. His work survives *because* it was refuted by Buddhists and Hindus alike — it was seen as a threat to *all* epistemological systems.

## 3. Ontology: Four Elements, No Ākāśa

- **Four elements**: earth, water, fire, air (catuḥ-bhūta)
- **No fifth element** (ākāśa/space) — distinguishes from Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika and Buddhist four-element models
- **Consciousness**: Emergent from material combination (like intoxication from fermented ingredients)
- **No soul (ātman), no afterlife, no karma, no rebirth**

---

## 4. Ethics: Hedonism or Pragmatism?

The "hedonism" charge is a **Brahminical distortion**. The famous verse:

> *"yāvaj jīvet sukhaṃ jīvet, ṛṇaṃ kṛtvā ghṛtaṃ pibet"*
> (Live happily as long as you live, drink ghee even if in debt)

**Appears in Madhava's hostile account, not in authentic Cārvāka fragments.**

**Actual ethical position**: Materialist pragmatism — maximize wellbeing in this life because there is no afterlife, no karma, no rebirth. Not "eat, drink, be merry" but "this life is all there is; make it good."

---

## 5. Why No Institutional Survival?

Unlike Buddhism and Jainism, Cārvāka/Lokayata had:

| Factor | Buddhism/Jainism | Cārvāka |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| **Monastic institution** | Saṅgha, organized lineage | None |
| **Royal patronage** | Post-Gupta kings favored | None post-Gupta |
| **Suppression** | Tolerated/patronized | Manusmṛti legalizes exclusion of nāstikas |
| **Absorption** | Distinct identity maintained | Absorbed into Tantra; lost distinct identity |

---

## 6. Pre-Cārvāka Materialism

**5-element system** (earth, water, fire, air, space) referenced in **Maṇimēkalai** (~6th c. CE, Tamil). This suggests a *pre-Cārvāka* materialist tradition in South India that included ākāśa, later reduced to four elements in classical Cārvāka.

**Ajita Keśakambala** (contemporary of Buddha, ~5th c. BCE) — earliest named materialist in Buddhist texts (Sāmaññaphala Sutta). Denied karma, rebirth, afterlife.
