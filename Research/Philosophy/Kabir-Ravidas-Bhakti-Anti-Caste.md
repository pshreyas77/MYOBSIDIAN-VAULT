# Kabir & Ravidas — Bhakti Anti-Caste Lineage

**For future Claude:** Fills Philosophy MOC Gap #3. Critical distinction: Only the Nirguṇa strand of Bhakti is anti-caste. Saguṇa Bhakti (Tulsidas, Mīrābāī) is devotional but caste-affirming. Kabir was OBC (weaver), not Dalit; Ravidas was Dalit (Chamar).

---

## 1. Kabir (1398–1448): The Radical Iconoclast

### Identity & Caste
- **Caste**: Julaha (weaver) — Muslim birth, Hindu upbringing
- **Identity contested**: Appropriated by Hindu right, Muslims, Dalits, secularists — all selectively
- **Critical note**: Recent scholarship (Hawley, Vaudeville, Lorenzen) notes his community was relatively prosperous weavers with some social mobility. His anti-caste rhetoric was more anti-Brahminical than pro-Dalit.

### Texts
| Text | Status | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| **Bijak** | Kabir Panth scripture | Most authentic collection |
| **Guru Granth Sahib** | 41 verses | Sikh scripture |
| **Panchvāṇī** | Dadupanth collection | |
| **Kabir Granthāvalī** | Modern scholarly reconstruction | |

### Core Philosophy
- **Nirguṇa Bhakti**: Formless divine (nirguṇa Brahman) accessible without priestly mediation
- **Anti-ritualism**: Rejected idol worship, pilgrimage, fasting, sacred thread, Quran, Vedas
- **Anti-caste**: *"Jaṭi-pāṭī pūchhe nahiṅ koī, hari ko bhaje so hari kā hoī"* (No one asks caste; whoever worships Hari belongs to Hari)
- **Syncretic idiom**: Used both Hindu (Rama, Hari) and Muslim (Allah, Khuda, Pir) names for the divine

### Political Appropriation
| Group | Claim | Reality |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Hindu right | "Hindu reformer" | Ignores Muslim idiom |
| Muslim | Sufi saint | Ignores rejection of Islam |
| Dalit movements | Anti-caste icon | He was OBC, not Dalit |
| Secularists | "Synthesis" | Ignores radical rejection of both traditions |

## 2. Ravidas (1450–1520): The Devotional Humanist

### Identity & Caste
- **Caste**: Chamar (leatherworker) — Dalit by birth, untouchable status
- **Untouchable status**: Central to his lived experience and philosophy

### Texts
| Text | Status | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| **Guru Granth Sahib** | 40 verses | Largest single collection |
| **Ravidassia Gutka** | Community scripture | |
| **Amritbani** | 2009 compilation | Ravidassia religion movement |

### Core Philosophy
- **Begumpura**: Utopian vision of a city without caste, tax, sorrow, or domination. **Not otherworldly** — a this-worldly moral ideal
- **Dignity of labor**: Elevated leatherwork as spiritual practice
- **Anti-Brahminism**: Rejected priestly mediation, ritual purity, Vedic authority
- **Theistic Nirguṇa**: Devotion to formless divine through loving surrender (bhakti), not Kabir's confrontational skepticism

### Modern Political Significance
- **Ravidassia Separate Religion Movement (2009)**: Followers broke from Sikhism after Vienna temple shooting (2009). Declared Ravidassia as separate religion with own scripture (Amritbani), symbol (Harr Nishaan), and rituals.
- **Dalit politics**: Ravidas claimed by BSP, Azad Samaj Party, and various Dalit cultural movements as foundational icon

## 3. Comparative: Kabir vs Ravidas

| Dimension | Kabir | Ravidas |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Caste** | Julaha (weaver, OBC) | Chamar (leatherworker, Dalit) |
| **Tone** | Confrontational, sarcastic, paradoxical | Humble, devotional, utopian |
| **Social target** | Both Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy | Primarily Brahminical caste hierarchy |
| **Divine concept** | Formless, beyond all names | Formless but accessible through loving devotion |
| **Key metaphor** | Weaver's loom (divine weaving) | Begumpura (utopian city) |
| **Legacy institution** | Kabir Panth (relatively small) | Ravidassia religion (2009 separation), Dalit politics |

## 4. Critical Update: Not All Bhakti Was Anti-Caste

Your vault should distinguish:

| Category | Figures | Stance |
|----------|---------|--------|
| **Nirguṇa anti-caste Bhakti** | Kabir, Ravidas, Dadu, Paltu | Rejected caste and ritual |
| **Saguṇa Brahminical Bhakti** | Tulsidas, Surdas, Mīrābāī | Devotional but caste-affirming or caste-indifferent |
| **Saguṇa non-Brahmin Bhakti** | Nāyanārs, Āḷvārs | Tamil devotional, often anti-Jain/Buddhist but not consistently anti-caste |

**Only the Nirguṇa strand belongs in your "anti-caste lineage."** The rest is devotional but not emancipatory.
