# Digital Gardens

**Definition:** A digital garden is a personal knowledge base that is publicly accessible and evolves through continuous refinement — as opposed to traditional blogs where posts are time-stamped and rarely updated.

Vanessa Groot (2024): *"a personal knowledge base that is publicly accessible and evolves through continuous refinement."*

Unlike chronological blogging or social media feeds, digital gardens emphasize non-linear, evolving connections between ideas.

---

## Key Characteristics

1. **Evergreen content** — Notes that grow and change over time rather than static posts
2. **Combinatorial growth** — Ideas connect in non-linear ways, creating emergent patterns
3. **Public thinking** — The garden shows work-in-progress, not just polished conclusions
4. **Bidirectional links** — Every note can link to others and back; backlinks form a graph

---

## Technical Requirements

- Bidirectional links (backlinks)
- Graph visualization of note connections
- Version history for each note
- Search by concept, not just date

---

## Supporting Evidence

- **Maggie Appleton (2025):** 67% of digital gardeners report improved retention of complex concepts vs. linear note-taking
- **Personal Knowledge Management Institute (2026):** Digital gardens work best with daily refinement sessions of 15-20 minutes
