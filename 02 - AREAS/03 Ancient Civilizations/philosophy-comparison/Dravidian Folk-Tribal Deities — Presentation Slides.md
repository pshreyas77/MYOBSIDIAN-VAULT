# SLIDE 2 — WHAT IS SANSKRITIZATION?

## The Analytical Framework

### M.N. Srinivas, 1952
*"The process by which a 'low' Hindu caste, or tribal or other group, attempts to rise higher in the ritual and social hierarchy by adopting the customs, rituals, beliefs, and way of life of a 'high' Brahminical Hindu caste."*

### Key terms used in this presentation:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Absorbed** | Fully integrated into Brahminical Hindu pantheon — given Sanskrit name, Puranic backstory, Brahmin priests |
| **Partially absorbed** | Sanskritization overlays added but core tribal ritual preserved |
| **Not absorbed** | Genuine survival from pre-Brahminical era — no or minimal Sanskritization |
| **Actively resisting** | Community explicitly refusing integration — political or religious reform movement |
| **Dual-track** | Same deity name exists in two parallel forms — folk + Sanskritized — simultaneously |

# SLIDE 3 — WHAT PREDICTS ABSORPTION VS. RESISTANCE?

## Five Factors That Determined Outcomes

| Factor | Deities That Got Absorbed | Deities That Resisted |
|--------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| **Proximity to Brahminical centers** | Near temple towns, urban Brahmin settlements | Remote forest/hill villages |
| **Agricultural system** | Wet-rice agrarian (Tamil plains, Kerala coastal) | Pastoral/shifting cultivation (Gond hills, Lambanis) |
| **Political patronage** | Royal patronage from Chola, Hoysala, Kakatiya kingdoms | No royal patronage; tribal political autonomy |
| **Timing of contact** | Early contact (pre-500 CE) → heavy absorption | Late contact (post-1500 CE) → selective or none |
| **Priesthood structure** | Brahmin priesthood adopted | Non-Brahmin/hereditary caste priest maintained |

### The Paradox of Exclusion
In Kerala, the Thiyyar community was *excluded* from Brahminical Hinduism — never given access to Brahmin temples. Paradoxically, this *preserved* their Theyyam and Muthappan traditions intact. Exclusion became preservation.

# SLIDE 5 — TAMIL NADU: THE DIVINE LINEAGE

## 12 Deities — Bhakti Movement Created a "Tamil Hindu" Third Path

**Key mechanism:** The Tamil Bhakti movement (6th–9th c. CE) — Nayanmars (Shaiva) and Alvars (Vaishnava) — created a parallel Tamil-language religious tradition. This gave folk deities a path into the Sanskritic fold *without Brahminical mediation* — via Tamil saints, Tamil scripture, Tamil ritual.

### Fully Absorbed Into Sanskritized Hindu Pantheon:

| Deity | Original Tribal Form | Sanskritized As | Evidence | Sanskritization Mechanism |
|-------|---------------------|----------------|----------|--------------------------|
| **Murugan** (Cheyyon) | Tamil Kurinji landscape god; associated with hills, arrows, Vel | **Skanda / Kartikeya** | Sangam corpus (300 BCE–300 CE) — HIGH | Fused with Sanskrit epic deity via Gupta-era texts; Sanskrit name adopted at folk level |
| **Mariamman** | Irula / Kurumba rain and smallpox goddess; pre-Sangam | **Parvati / Kali / Durga / Draupadi** | 10th c. Chola bronze — HIGH | 10th c. Chola temples; Shakta synthesis; Brahmin priests now serve alongside Irula/Kurumba keepers |
| **Ayyanar** (Ayyanappan) | Kootrall Vazhi guardian; hero stone tradition; 3rd c. CE | **Sastha / Hariharasuta** | Hero stones, Arcot inscriptions — HIGH | Brahmanda Purana (7th c. CE) linked to Shiva; Pottr (potter) caste still primary priest |
| **Madurai Veeran** | Sangam hero stone tradition (Vellalar); 2nd–1st c. BCE | **Guardian deity / Bhairava** | Sangam-era viragal — MODERATE | Hero stones → village deity → Shaiva absorption; linked to Kshatriya hero narrative |
| **Muniswara** (Muneeshwaran) | Tamil plains hero stone tradition; pre-Sangam | **Shiva (Mahadeva)** | Hero stone tradition; Sangam mentions — MODERATE | Shaiva absorption; some Brahmin priests added later but village-level non-Brahmin ritual survives |
| **Sudalai Madan** | Southern TN (Chakkiliya community); medieval | **Bhairava** (fierce Shiva form) | Medieval shrine inscriptions — MODERATE | Shaiva absorption via fierce deity classification |
| **Kadambari Amman** | Billava community; Kongu region (NW TN) | **Shakti / Durga** | Hoysala temple inscriptions — MODERATE | Absorbed into Tamil Shakta pantheon; Billava community priest maintained |
| **Tirumala Viravan** | Tirumala hills / Chittoor region | **Vishnu / Venkateswara** | Oral tradition — LOW-MODERATE | Vaishnava absorption; Brahmin priests at main Tirumala temple; village shrines non-Brahmin |

### Partially Absorbed (Non-Brahmin Ritual Preserved):

| Deity | What Was Absorbed | What Remains Intact | Confidence |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Karuppasamy** | Linked to Ayyanar as guardian pair; named in Sanskritic registers | Non-Brahmin Pariah/BC priest; oral tradition | MODERATE |
| **Poonachi Amman** | Associated with Shakta name at regional temples | Village-level only; no inscriptional anchor | LOW-MODERATE |

# SLIDE 6 — KERALA: THEYAM AND THE EXCLUSION PARADOX

## 9 Deities — Excluded Communities Preserved Pre-Hindu Traditions

**Key mechanism:** Kerala's matrilineal property system and Nair intermediate caste created a distinct power structure. The Thiyyar community was *excluded* from Brahminical Hinduism — never given access to Brahmin temples. They never needed to Sanskritize to gain social status, so they never did.

### The Exclusion Paradox = Preservation
> Because Thiyyar were kept OUT of Brahminical Hinduism, Theyyam and Muthappan rituals remained entirely non-Brahminical. If they had been allowed in, they would have adopted Brahminical forms to rise in status — like in Tamil Nadu. Exclusion preserved what inclusion would have destroyed.

### Partially / Marginally Absorbed:

| Deity | What Was Absorbed | What Remains Intact | Confidence |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Ayyappan** (Sastha) | Sabarimala pilgrimage from 12th c.; Sanskrit Brahmanda Purana; now has Brahmin/Nair priests | Original tribal claim disputed by historians Gurukkal & Narayanan; 855 CE Padmanabhapuram inscription shows Sastha as subordinate | LOW-MODERATE |
| **Vishnumoorthy Theyyam** | 13th c. Hoysala Vaishnava absorption; linked to Vishnu | Non-Brahmin Kalanaadi performers; tribal ritual core intact | MODERATE |
| **Theyyam pantheon (~456 types)** | Shaiva/Shakta overlays added post-13th c. Hoysala period | Blood sacrifice, toddy, trance possession — all core ritual intact; Thiyyar community maintained full autonomy | HIGH (ethnographic) |
| **Kuttichathan** (Chathan) | Associated with Bhairava (Shaiva) | Non-Brahmin Pisharodi community priest | LOW-MODERATE |

### NOT Absorbed — Control Cases:

| Deity | What Makes It "Not Absorbed" | Evidence | Confidence |
|-------|------------------------------|----------|------------|
| **Muthappan** (Thirvappana + Vellatom) | No idol. No *kodimaram* (temple flagstaff). Toddy/fish/flesh offerings. Inclusive of ALL castes and religions. Performed year-round via Theyyam. | Parassinikkadavu temple 17th c.; Kolathiri royal references; oral tradition | MODERATE-HIGH |
| **Kattunayakan shamanic** | Shamanic tradition; no Brahmin mediation; community autonomy intact | Edgar Thurston 1909; recent anthropological fieldwork | LOW-MODERATE |
| **Padayani tradition** | Blood sacrifice and trance; associated with Bhagavathi/Kali but parallel tradition | Distinct from Theyyam; Central Travancore; pre-Hindu | LOW-MODERATE |

### Key Stat: Theyyam
- **456 documented types** (K.K.N. Kurup, anthropological study)
- Ancient: Neolithic / Chalcolithic (Bridget & Raymond Alchin)
- Non-Brahminical castes only: Thiyyar, Malayar, Vannan, Pulayar, Mavilan
- Ritual elements that would shock Brahminical Hinduism: blood sacrifice, toddy offerings, trance possession

# SLIDE 7 — KARNATAKA: THE LINGAYAT RESISTANCE

## 10 Deities — Basava Created South India's Strongest Counter-Case

**Key mechanism:** The Hoysala dynasty (12th–14th c.) patronized both Brahminical Vaishnavism AND local folk traditions simultaneously. This dual patronage meant: (a) local deities got royal legitimacy (accelerating Sanskritization), BUT (b) community ritual forms were tolerated alongside formal temples (preventing total Brahminization).

### Absorbed Into Sanskritized Hindu Pantheon:

| Deity | Sanskritized As | Evidence | Mechanism |
|-------|----------------|----------|-----------|
| **Khandoba** (Mailara) | **Martanda Bhairava** (Shiva + Surya) | Inscriptions 1063 CE + 1148 CE — HIGH; *Malhari Mahatmya* composed ~1460–1510 CE | Sanskrit *Mahatmya* specifically commissioned to legitimize; linked to Shiva (Martanda = solar form of Shiva) and Bhairava |
| **Karaga** (Tigala community) | **Draupadi** (Mahabharata heroine) | Oral tradition; folklore — LOW-MODERATE | Sanskritization via epic association; Tigala women perform Draupadi-worship as village guardian |

### Marginally Absorbed:

| Deity | What Was Absorbed | What Remains Intact | Confidence |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Bhuta Kola** | Some deified heroes linked to Shiva | Spirit-possession ritual kept distinct; Tuluva community autonomy | MODERATE |
| **Kuruba folk deities** | Linked to Shiva and Grama Devata | Kuruba community priest; pastoral identity | LOW-MODERATE |
| **Idiga folk gods** | Partially linked to Shiva | Idiga community priest | LOW |

### NOT Absorbed — Special Cases:

| Deity | What Makes It "Not Absorbed" | Evidence | Confidence |
|-------|------------------------------|----------|------------|
| **Basava / Lingayat (Vīraśaiva)** | **COUNTER-CASE — ACTIVE REJECTION** | 12th c. (1131–1167 CE) | Historical; Basava's own *vācana*s in Kannada; still distinct from Hinduism |

**Why Basava/Lingayat is the strongest resistance case in South India:**
- Founded by Basaveshwara (1131–1167 CE) — reform from WITHIN the tradition
- Rejected: Brahminical temple worship, Sanskrit scripture, caste hierarchy, animal sacrifice at temples
- Created instead: portable *īṣṭaliṅga* (personal linga worn on body), Kannada-language vācana scripture, open priesthood (any Lingayat can serve), rejection of temple-based mediation between worshipper and god
- Remains separate from Hinduism to this day — Lingayats are counted as a separate community in the Indian census
- **Key difference from passive survival:** This was an active intellectual and social reform movement with clear philosophical rejection of Brahminical authority

# SLIDE 8 — ANDHRA PRADESH: THE DUAL-TRACK PHENOMENON

## 10 Deities — Renuka Exists in Two Parallel Forms Simultaneously

**Key mechanism:** The Kakatiya dynasty (12th–14th c.) was Shaiva and promoted both Sanskritized Shaivism AND local folk traditions. Royal patronage given to folk deities paradoxically *accelerated* their Sanskritization — because rulers needed Brahminical legitimacy, and Sanskritization was the price of that legitimacy.

### Fully Absorbed:

| Deity | Sanskritized As | Evidence | Mechanism |
|-------|----------------|----------|-----------|
| **Mallanna** (Mallikarjuna) | **Mallikarjuna / Jyotirlinga (Shiva)** | Warangal inscriptions 1369 CE — HIGH | Kakatiya royal patronage → merged with Shiva as Jyotirlinga; Brahmin priests now at main temple |
| **Gangamma** (Rayalaseema) | **Shakti / Kali** | Temple records 16th c. — MODERATE | Regional temple development; absorbed into Hindu Shakta tradition |
| **Renuka (Sanskritized form)** | **Adi Shakti / Parashakti** | Mahabharata, Harivamsa, Bhagavata Purana — HIGH (Puranic) | Puranic integration; wife of Jamadagni, mother of Parashurama; one of 4 Adi Shakti incarnations |

### Partially / Marginally Absorbed:

| Deity | What Was Absorbed | What Remains Intact | Confidence |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Maisamma** (Telangana/AP border) | Sanskrit Puranic overlay: "Daughter of Shiva and Parvati" | Multiple variants (Katta, Nalla, Tella, Bangaaru, Vendi, Panta); non-Brahmin herders | LOW-MODERATE |
| **Chenchu tribal deities** | Some linked to Venkateswara (Tirumala) via proximity | Distinct Chenchu jungle deities; Chenchu community-only worship; no Brahmin mediation | LOW-MODERATE |
| **Konda Kapu tribal deities** | Some linking to local Hindu forms | Tribal religious autonomy maintained; tribal shamans | LOW-MODERATE |

# SLIDE 9 — ANDHRA PRADESH: THE RENUKA DUAL-TRACK

## The Same Deity Name, Two Completely Different Traditions

> **This is one of the most important findings in this research — and one of the most underappreciated phenomena in Indian religious history.**

### Renuka / Yellamma — Two Tracks, One Name

| Aspect | Folk Form | Sanskritized (Puranic) Form |
|--------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Who worships** | Malas, Madigas (Dalit communities) in rural Karnataka/AP/Telangana | Brahminical Hindu households across South India |
| **Priesthood** | Non-Brahmin; often Malas/Madigas themselves | Brahmin priests exclusively |
| **Origin myth** | Oral tradition; village-specific origin myths; independent of Vedic/Puranic narrative | Wife of Jamadagni, mother of Parashurama (Mahabharata); one of 4 Adi Shakti incarnations |
| **Ritual practice** | Village shrine or open ground; sometimes no permanent temple; blood sacrifice, toddy | Brahminical puja; Sanskrit chants; temple with full iconography |
| **Social meaning** | Dalit community deity; marker of Dalit identity and autonomy | Brahminical Shakti worship; part of Sanskritic Hindu mainstream |
| **Sanskritization path** | NONE — maintained independently | Fully Sanskritized via Puranic integration |

### Why This Matters
If you only studied Renuka in her Puranic form, you would conclude she is entirely absorbed into Brahminical Hinduism. If you only studied her folk form, you would conclude she resisted entirely. **Both are true simultaneously.** The dual-track pattern is extremely common across South India — Renuka is just the clearest documented case.

# SLIDE 10 — TELANGANA: GRAMA DEVATA CLUSTER

## 12 Deities — Two-Level Absorption and Active Political Resistance

**Key mechanism:** Telangana's forest geography and Gond/tribal communities' political autonomy (until 19th c. British land acquisition) created conditions for *late* Brahminical contact. Less time for Sanskritization pressure. But two-level absorption complicates the picture: big regional temples have Brahmin/OBC priests and Sanskritization overlays, while village-level shrines are priest-less and nearly intact.

### Two-Level Absorption Pattern
> The same deity name (Pochamma) can mean "absorbed" at the big temple and "not absorbed" at the village shrine 2 km away. These are the same deity — different levels of the same tradition, existing simultaneously.

| Temple Level | Village Level |
|-------------|---------------|
| Brahmin or OBC priest | Often no permanent priest — village elder or woman officiates |
| Sanskrit name used | Oral folk name used |
| Temple building with icon | Sacred site may be just a spot, a tree, or a rock |
| Linked to Kali/Durga/Devi in religious registers | No mediation sought from Brahminical texts |

### Fully Absorbed:
| Deity | Sanskritized As | Evidence | Mechanism |
|-------|----------------|----------|-----------|
| **Mallanna** (shared with Karnataka) | **Mallikarjuna / Jyotirlinga** | Warangal 1369 CE — MODERATE-STRONG | Kakatiya royal patronage → Shaiva absorption |

### Partially / Marginally Absorbed:
| Deity | What Was Absorbed | What Remains Intact | Confidence |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Pochamma / Poleramma / Ellamma / Peddamma** | Name loosely linked to Kali/Durga/Devi at big temples | Village shrines: priest-less, non-Brahmin, village elder/woman officiates; village-level nearly intact | MODERATE |
| **Danteshwari** (Gond, extends from Bastar) | Declared state goddess of Chhattisgarh (2001); name in Hindu registers | Tribal ritual core preserved; tribal priest; Gond communities do not accept Shiva/Vishnu parallel | MODERATE |

# SLIDE 11 — TELANGANA: GOND PERSA PEN — ACTIVE RESISTANCE

## The Formless Clan Deity That Refuses All Integration

**The single most important resistance case in Telangana.**

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Name** | Persa Pen ( Gondi: *persa* = god/divine; *pen* = clan) |
| **Meaning** | "The clan's own god" — explicitly NOT a Hindu god, not a village god, not a regional god |
| **Form** | Formless. No idol. No temple. Ever. |
| **Sacred object** | Sacred wooden pillar only — called *kamk* |
| **Worship** | Oral tradition, trance, ritual dance; Gond tribal only |
| **Priesthood** | Gond tribal — men and women participate equally; no Brahmin mediation ever |
| **Active refusal** | Gond elders explicitly refuse integration with Shiva, Baradeo, or any Hindu deity. Active political resistance via Tribal Welfare Research Institute (TWRI) |
| **Evidence** | TWRI field documentation; oral tradition; no inscriptional anchor — but HIGH as a resistance case |
| **State spread** | Telangana (Adilabad district), Odisha, Madhya Pradesh |

### Why This Is Different From Passive Survival
Most unabsorbed deities survived because Brahminical Hinduism never reached them — remote geography, late contact, etc. Gond Persa Pen is different: Gond communities *have been* approached by Hinduization efforts. They have *actively and collectively refused*. The political assertion of Persa Pen as a specifically Gond deity is a modern identity movement, not passive geographic survival.

# SLIDE 13 — RESISTANCE COUNTER-CASES: THE BIG FOUR

## Deities and Traditions That Did NOT Get Absorbed

These four cases are the most important findings in this research — they prove Sanskritization was not uniform, inevitable, or total.

### 1. Lingayat / Vīraśaiva (Karnataka, 12th c.)
- **Not passive survival — active reform rejection**
- Basava (1131–1167 CE) created an alternative religious system
- Portable *īṣṭaliṅga*, Kannada scripture (*vācana*), open priesthood
- Explicitly rejected: Brahminical authority, Sanskrit scripture, temple worship hierarchy
- Still separate from Hinduism in Indian census today

### 2. Gond Persa Pen (Telangana/Odisha/MP)
- **Formless clan deity; no idol; no temple; sacred wooden pillar (*kamk*)**
- Gond elders explicitly refuse integration with Shiva/Baradeo
- Active political resistance — Tribal Welfare Research Institute documentation
- Community identity marker; political assertion, not passive survival

### 3. Sarna Religion (Jharkhand, Chota Nagpur)
- **Named, distinct religion — not Hinduism**
- Separate census enumeration in India (Sarna option on census forms)
- Sacred grove theology: *Jaher Than* — jal (water), jaṅgal (forest), jamīn (land)
- Oraon, Munda, Santal communities; Birsa Munda movement (1875–1900); Tana Bhagat (1914)
- Communities explicitly choose Sarna as their religion in census

### 4. Muthappan (Kerala)
- **No idol; no *kodimaram*; toddy/fish/flesh; inclusive of ALL castes/religions**
- Thiyyar community ritual only — never Brahmin
- Parassinikkadavu temple 17th c. but ritual form unchanged from pre-temple era
- Exclusion paradox: being kept out of Brahminical Hinduism preserved it entirely

# SLIDE 14 — MODERN DESANSKRITIZATION MOVEMENTS

## Reverse Pressure: Dravidian Nationalism, Periyarism, Ambedkar Buddhism, Sarna

These are cases where *already-Sanskritized* or *partially-Sanskritized* traditions are being actively pushed back — not by Brahminical forces, but by reform movements from within or below.

| Movement | State | Mechanism | What Changed |
|----------|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Periyarist rationalism** (Self-Respect Movement) | Tamil Nadu | Public burns of sacred threads; anti-temple rationalism; Periyar invited Ambedkar | Educated Dravidian nationalists reduced folk-deity observance; some rural deities survived |
| **DMK Tamil-only cultural movement** | Tamil Nadu | Sanskritized Hindu temples framed as North Indian imposition; pre-Brahminical Tamil identity promoted | Mariamman, Ayyanar reframed as Tamil-only, not absorbed into Sanskrit pantheon |
| **Ambedkar's Buddhism conversion** (1956) | Maharashtra, TN, Karnataka, National | 500,000+ Dalits converted to Navayana Buddhism; explicitly rejected Brahminical Hindu deity hierarchy | Dalit communities reject folk-deity worship as Brahminical contamination; radical desanskritization |
| **Varkari movement** (13th–18th c.) | Maharashtra | Sant Tukaram and Eknath created pan-caste devotional tradition in Marathi; abhangs in local language | Neither Sanskritization (imposed from above) nor rejection — *democratized from below*; Vithoba worshipped by Brahmin and non-Brahmin equally |
| **Sarna political assertion** | Jharkhand | 1930s Oraon activists; Jharkhand movement; separate census identity | Gond, Oraon, Santal communities explicitly refuse Hindu absorption for tribal deities |
| **Gond political assertion** | Odisha, Telangana, Maharashtra | Persa Pen, Danteshwari as Gond identity markers; TWRI documentation; Gond MLAs refuse temple integration | Active political resistance; tribal deities as ethnic identity anchors |

### The Varkari Exception
> Vithoba / Pandharpur is neither Sanskritization nor rejection — it is *democratization from below*. The Varkari movement (13th c. Maharashtra) made Vaishnava devotion accessible to all castes through Marathi-language abhangs. Lower castes participated on their terms, without adopting Brahminical customs. This is a third pattern beyond absorption and resistance.

# SLIDE 18 — SUMMARY

## What This Atlas Tells Us

### Key Finding 1: Sanskritization Was Selective, Not Total
Brahminical Hinduism absorbed many deities — but by no means all. Of 63+ traditions documented, approximately 22 are fully absorbed, ~25 are partially absorbed, and ~15-16 show genuine resistance or non-absorption. The assumption of uniform Brahminical conquest is wrong.

### Key Finding 2: Resistance Came From Two Directions
Passive survival (geographic remoteness, late contact) explains some unabsorbed traditions. But the most important cases — Lingayat, Gond Persa Pen, Sarna — are **active** resistance or **political assertion**. Brahminical Hinduism was not inevitable; it was sometimes refused.

### Key Finding 3: Exclusion Sometimes Preserved
The Kerala exclusion paradox is counterintuitive but important: communities kept OUT of Brahminical Hinduism never needed to Sanskritize to gain status, so they never did. The Thiyyar community's ritual traditions survived precisely because they were excluded.

### Key Finding 4: Dual-Track is the Norm, Not the Exception
Renuka/Yellamma is the clearest documented case, but the dual-track pattern — folk form + Puranic form coexisting — is widespread. Any analysis that looks at only one track will get the picture wrong.

### Key Finding 5: Modern Desanskritization is Real
Periyarism, DMK Tamil-only culture, Ambedkar Buddhism, Varkari, and Sarna assertion are not marginal phenomena — they represent tens of millions of Indians actively rejecting or modifying Sanskritization. This is a live, ongoing process.
