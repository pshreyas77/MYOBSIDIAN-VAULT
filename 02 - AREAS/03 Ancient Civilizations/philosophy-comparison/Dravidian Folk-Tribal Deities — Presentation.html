Framework

What Is Sanskritization?

"The process by which a 'low' Hindu caste, or tribal or other group, attempts to rise higher in the ritual and social hierarchy by adopting the customs, rituals, beliefs, and way of life of a 'high' Brahminical Hindu caste." — M.N. Srinivas, Religion and Society Among the Coorgs of South India (1952)

Key Terms in This Presentation

TermMeaning
ABSORBEDFully integrated into Brahminical Hindu pantheon — Sanskrit name, Puranic backstory, Brahmin priests
PARTIALLY ABSORBEDSanskritization overlays added but core tribal ritual preserved at community level
NOT ABSORBEDGenuine survival from pre-Brahminical era — no or minimal Sanskritization contact
ACTIVELY RESISTINGCommunity explicitly refusing integration — reform movement or political assertion
DUAL-TRACKSame deity name exists in two parallel forms — folk + Sanskritized — simultaneously

Pattern Analysis

Five Factors That Determined Outcomes

FactorAbsorbedResisted
Proximity to Brahminical centersNear temple towns, urban Brahmin settlementsRemote forest / hill / desert villages
Agricultural systemWet-rice agrarian (Tamil plains, Kerala coastal)Pastoral / shifting cultivation (Gond hills, Lambanis)
Political patronageRoyal patronage from Chola, Hoysala, Kakatiya kingdomsNo royal patronage; tribal political autonomy preserved
Timing of Brahminical contactEarly contact (pre-500 CE) → heavy absorptionLate contact (post-1500 CE) → selective or none
Priesthood structureBrahmin priesthood adopted → absorption acceleratedNon-Brahmin / hereditary caste priest maintained
The Exclusion Paradox (Kerala): Thiyyar communities were excluded from Brahminical Hinduism — never given access to Brahmin temples. They never needed to Sanskritize to gain social status, so they never did. Exclusion preserved what inclusion would have destroyed.

Karnataka · 10 Deities

Karnataka: Lingayat — The Strongest Resistance Case in South India

The Basava/Lingayat counter-case — active rejection, not passive survival.
Founded by Basaveshwara (1131–1167 CE): rejected Brahminical temple worship, Sanskrit scripture, caste hierarchy. Created: portable īṣṭaliṅga (personal linga worn on body), Kannada vācana scripture, open priesthood. Still separate from Hinduism in Indian census today.

Andhra Pradesh · 10 Deities

Andhra Pradesh: Renuka Dual-Track Phenomenon

Key mechanism: Kakatiya dynasty (12th–14th c.) patronized both Sanskritized Shaivism AND local folk traditions. Royal patronage accelerated Sanskritization — because rulers needed Brahminical legitimacy, and Sanskritization was the price.

Renuka / Yellamma — One Name, Two Parallel Traditions

AspectFolk FormSanskritized (Puranic) Form
Who worshipsMalas, Madigas (Dalit communities) rural Karnataka/AP/TelanganaBrahminical Hindu households across South India
PriesthoodNon-Brahmin; often Malas/Madigas themselvesBrahmin priests exclusively
Origin mythOral tradition; village-specific; independent of Puranic narrativeWife of Jamadagni, mother of Parashurama (Mahabharata); one of 4 Adi Shakti incarnations
RitualVillage shrine or open ground; blood sacrifice, toddyBrahminical puja; Sanskrit chants; temple with iconography
Social meaningDalit community deity; marker of Dalit identityBrahminical Shakti worship; mainstream Hindu tradition
Sanskritization pathNONE — maintained independentlyFully Sanskritized via Puranic integration

If you only studied Renuka in her Puranic form, you conclude she is entirely absorbed. Folk form alone says she resisted entirely. Both are true simultaneously.

Fully Absorbed

  • Mallanna → Mallikarjuna / Jyotirlinga (Shiva) · HIGH · Warangal 1369 CE · Kakatiya royal patronage
  • Gangamma (Rayalaseema) → Shakti / Kali · MOD · Temple records 16th c.

Telangana: Two-Level Absorption and Gond Persa Pen

Two-level absorption: The same deity name (Pochamma) means "absorbed" at the big regional temple and "not absorbed" at the village shrine 2 km away. Same deity — different levels of the same tradition, coexisting.

Partially / Marginally Absorbed

Telangana · Active Political Resistance

Gond Persa Pen — Formless Clan Deity That Refuses All Integration

The single most important resistance case in Telangana.
Gond communities in Adilabad district (Telangana), Odisha, and MP worship a deity explicitly, politically, and collectively not a Hindu god.
AttributeDetail
NamePersa Pen — Gondi: persa = god/divine; pen = clan. "The clan's own god"
FormFormless. No idol. No temple. Ever.
Sacred objectSacred wooden pillar — called kamk
WorshipOral tradition, trance, ritual dance; Gond tribal only
PriesthoodGond tribal — men and women equally; no Brahmin mediation ever
Active refusalGond elders explicitly refuse integration with Shiva, Baradeo, or any Hindu deity. Documented by Tribal Welfare Research Institute (TWRI)
EvidenceTWRI field documentation; oral tradition. HIGH as a resistance case
Why different from passive survivalMost unabsorbed deities survived because Brahminical Hinduism never reached them. Gond Persa Pen is different: communities have been approached by Hinduization and actively refused collectively. Political assertion, not passive geographic survival.

Jharkhand · Extended Dravidian Belt

Sarna Religion — India's Most Clearly Unabsorbed Tribal Religion

Sarna is the most clearly defined non-absorbed tribal religion in India. Named distinct identity, separate census enumeration, sacred grove theology, active political assertion. Oraon, Munda, and Santal communities explicitly choose "Sarna" as their religion in Indian census forms.

Gram DeotaVillage deityDistinct from Hindu grama devata
Marang BuruGreat MountainEarth/nature; Santal, Munda, Oraon
Chandi BongaWarrior goddessOraon/Munda; pre-Hindu

Deities NOT Absorbed Into Hinduism

  • Singbonga — Oraon sun; tribal Pahan priest. HIGH
  • Gram Deota — Sarna grove deity; distinct from Hindu pantheon. MOD
  • Chandi Bonga — Oraon/Munda warrior goddess. LOW

Historical Movements

  • Birsa Munda movement (1875–1900): Religious revival asserting tribal identity against colonial + Christian + Hindu pressure
  • Tana Bhagat (1914-): Oraon reform; refused taxes, alcohol, witchcraft; created syncretic Oraon identity
  • Jharkhand movement (1930s+): Sarna declared distinct religion; Oraon activists led the declaration

Key Findings

5 Conclusions This Atlas Changes

1 — Selective, Not Total: Of 63+ traditions: ~22 fully absorbed, ~25 partially, ~15–16 genuinely resisting. Uniform Brahminical conquest is wrong.
2 — Two Directions of Resistance: Passive survival (geography, late contact) explains some. But Lingayat, Gond Persa Pen, Sarna are active resistance or political assertion.
3 — Exclusion Sometimes Preserved: Kerala: Thiyyar excluded from Brahminical Hinduism — never needed to Sanskritize for status — so never did. Exclusion preserved what inclusion would have destroyed.
4 — Dual-Track Is the Norm: Renuka/Yellamma clearest case; folk + Puranic forms coexisting is widespread. Single-track analysis always gets it wrong.
5 — Modern Desanskritization Is Real: Periyarism, DMK Tamil-only, Ambedkar Buddhism, Varkari, Sarna — tens of millions actively modifying or rejecting Sanskritization. Live process, not just history.
