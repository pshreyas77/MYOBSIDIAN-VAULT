## Overview

The prostate is a walnut-sized organ in males that plays a major role in the production of semen. The easiest way to access the prostate is by way of an index finger carefully inserted into the rectum. The processes for accessing the prostate as part of a medical exam (which should be done by a doctor) or for sexual pleasure are the same, and the same precautions should be taken.

**Location**: In your rectum, between your penis and belly button.

---

## ⚠️ Important Medical Disclaimer

> **Medical professionals do not recommend prostate self-exams.** An "untrained finger" is unlikely to accurately recognize signs of problems, and there is a small but not inconsequential risk of damage to the rectum or prostate.
>
> Talk to your primary care doctor to determine if you should have a DRE (digital rectal exam) to check your prostate.
>
> **Check your prostate if you're**:
> - Older than 40
> - Have a high risk for prostate cancer
> - Have symptoms of an enlarged or infected prostate

## Method 1: Touching Your Prostate with Your Finger

### Step 1: See a Trained Professional for Medical Examination
- Medical professionals do not recommend prostate self-exams
- Talk to your primary care doctor about DRE (digital rectal exam)
- If accessing for sexual pleasure, take all precautions and work slowly/gentle

### Step 2: Clean Thoroughly
- Take a shower and clean "between the cheeks" thoroughly
- Use soap, water, and a soft washcloth
- Rinse thoroughly with clean water
- **Don't** use rough washcloth, scrub vigorously, or clean deep into rectum
- Accept it's not possible to get 100% clean

### Step 3: Prepare Hands
- Trim nails and file sharp/jagged edges (especially index finger)
- Wash and dry hands
- Put on sterile exam glove on the hand you'll use
- Remove ring on index finger if wearing one

### Step 4: Apply Lubricant
- Use petroleum jelly or personal lubricant gel (e.g., KY Gel)
- Coat entire index finger from tip to at least middle knuckle
- Don't be shy about amount

### Step 5: Assume Comfortable Position
- **Clinical position**: Lie on side with knees brought to chest
- **Self-access alternative**: Stand up and lean forward so hips flex outward
- Choose what's comfortable for you

### Step 6: Relax
- Remain calm and relaxed
- Rectum naturally tenses during insertion
- Try relaxing music or deep breathing exercises beforehand

### Step 7: Insert Finger
- Insert tip of gloved, lubed index finger into rectum
- Work slowly and gently
- Stop once first knuckle is inside
- Use finger first few times (not devices) until comfortable

### Step 8: Aim Correctly
- Aim finger (without bending it) between belly button and penis
- Angle finger forward to access prostate
- Don't bend knuckles — adjust angle of entire finger

### Step 9: Make Contact
- Insert deeper until you make contact with prostate
- Middle knuckle will likely enter rectum before fingertip reaches prostate
- **On contact**: prostate feels like a firm, rounded bulb — distinct from surrounding tissue

## Method 2: Identifying Potential Prostate Problems

### Signs to Watch For
- **Frequent urination** (especially at night)
- **Difficulty starting/stopping urination**
- **Weak urine stream**
- **Pain/burning during urination/ejaculation**
- **Blood in urine/semen**
- **Pain in lower back, hips, or pelvic area**
- **Erectile dysfunction**

### When to Contact a Doctor
- Any of the above symptoms
- Age 40+ with family history
- Routine screening discussion with doctor

## Safety Precautions

| Precaution | Why |
|------------|-----|
| **Use sterile gloves** | Prevent infection |
| **Trim nails completely** | Prevent tissue damage |
| **Use plenty of lubricant** | Prevent tearing/discomfort |
| **Go slowly** | Allow sphincter to relax |
| **Stop if pain** | Prevent injury |
| **Don't force** | Risk of rectal/prostate damage |
| **Clean before and after** | Hygiene |
