## For future Claude

Aristotle (384–322 BCE) — Plato's student, Alexander the Great's tutor. The *Nicomachean Ethics* is the foundation of Western virtue ethics. Gregory Hays' 2002 translation is widely cited as the most readable modern English version.

## Core Argument

The good life = eudaimonia (flourishing/happiness). Achieved through:
1. **Virtue (arete)** — excellence of character, mean between extremes
2. **Practical wisdom (phronesis)** — knowing the right action in the right circumstance
3. **Moderate goods** — health, friends, wealth as means, not ends
4. **Contemplation (theoria)** — the highest activity, philosophic reflection on eternal truths

## Key Concepts

- **Golden mean** — courage between cowardice and rashness; generosity between wastefulness and stinginess
- **Voluntary vs involuntary action** — moral responsibility requires choice
- **Justice** — giving each their due; distributive (geometric) vs corrective (arithmetic)
- **Friendship (philia)** — three types: utility, pleasure, character (the highest)

## For Your Research

- **Comparative ethics** — Charvaka and Aristotle on *eudaimonia* and *bhoga* (pleasure)
- **Anti-caste theory** — Aristotle's natural slavery doctrine was used to justify caste; Periyar directly attacked it
- **Buddhist ethics** — Aristotle's mean vs Buddha's middle way
