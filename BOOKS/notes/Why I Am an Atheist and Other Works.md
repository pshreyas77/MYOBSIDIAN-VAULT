## For future Claude

Bhagat Singh (1907–1931) — executed by the British at age 23 for his role in the killing of British police officer John Saunders (mistaken for Scott, the killer of Lala Lajpat Rai). On death row he read voraciously — Nietzsche, Marx, Lenin, Bakunin, Kropotkin — and wrote essays that fused anti-colonialism, atheism, and revolutionary socialism. This collection includes:

- **Why I Am an Atheist** (October 5–6, 1930) — written on request from friends who said "you were an atheist even before" and now seek clarity
- **About God** — unfinished
- **Letter to Father** — on his mother's grief
- **The Problem** — unfinished
- **I Want to Live** — philosophical, unfinished
- Various letters and jail notes

## Core Argument of "Why I Am an Atheist"

Singh's argument is **not** the Western liberal "no evidence for God" position. It's a layered refutation:

1. **Belief in God presupposes belief in soul** — both are unverified
2. **Atheism as a duty, not just an intellectual position** — quoting Bertrand Russell and others, argues that *all* religious thought has been used to justify exploitation
3. **The poor man's faith vs the rich man's exploitation** — "The idea of God is helpful to the rich man, as it makes him think that his wealth is a divine gift and that he is a chosen one"
4. **Atheist ≠ immoral** — argues morality comes from social instinct, not divine command
5. **Sri Krishna, Mahavira, Buddha, Christ, Muhammad** — all taught that "no man can be saved till he has surrendered his ego" — i.e., a personal God is incompatible with social progress

The final passage: "Why I am an atheist? Because I do not believe in the existence of God. ... I am an atheist not because I want to be one. I am an atheist because I cannot bring myself to believe in a God."

## Why This Book Matters

For Shreyas's research focus on Indian philosophy + anti-caste thought, Bhagat Singh is the bridge figure between:
- **Pre-independence nationalism** (Gandhi, Tilak, etc.)
- **Revolutionary Marxism** (Lenin, Mao)
- **Atheist rationalism** (Periyar's Self-Respect movement started 1925)
- **Charvaka / Lokayata revival** — Singh's critique echoes Charvaka's "no soul, no afterlife"
