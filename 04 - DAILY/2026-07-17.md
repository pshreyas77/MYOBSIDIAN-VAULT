### Research Session: Language Families & Indian Political Geography

#### 1. **Dravidian Language Family** (Cross-checked from [[05 - MAPS/Historical Linguistics MOC]] + Wikipedia)
- **~75–80 languages**, ~250M speakers, **endemic to South Asia**
- **4 branches**: South I (Tamil-Kannada), South II (Telugu-Kui), Central (Gondi-Kui), North (Brahui/Kurukh/Malto)
- **Founding ancestor**: Proto-Dravidian (~4,000–3,000 BCE) — **never written**, possibly Indus Valley language
- **Earliest attested**: Old Tamil (3rd c. BCE Tamil-Brahmi) → Sangam Tamil (300 BCE – 200 CE)
- **Classical languages**: Tamil (2004), Kannada (2008), Telugu (2008), Malayalam (2013)
- **Indus Script** (~2,600–1,900 BCE) — undeciphered, may be early Dravidian or Indo-Aryan
- **NOT older than Sumerian/Egyptian** — those have written records from ~3,200–3,300 BCE

#### 2. **Indo-Aryan Language Family** (Cross-checked from [[05 - MAPS/Historical Linguistics MOC]] + Wikipedia)
- **~200+ languages**, ~800M+ speakers
- **Indo-European → Indo-Iranian → Indo-Aryan** migration to India ~3,500 years ago
- **Branches**: Western Pahari, Eastern Pahari, Northern, Western, Central, Eastern, Northwestern, Dardic, Sinhalese-Maldivian
- **Major Indian languages**: Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia, Assamese, Maithili, Bhojpuri, Kashmiri, Nepali
- **Classical**: Sanskrit (2005), Odia (2014)

#### 3. **Sino-Tibetan Language Family** (Cross-checked from Wikipedia)
- **~400–500 languages**, ~1.4B speakers
- **Sinitic (Chinese)** + **Tibeto-Burman** + Karenic, Bai, Tani, Sal, Kuki-Chin, Tamangic, Tibetic, Himalayish, Lolo-Burmese-Naxi
- **NE India coverage**: Tani (Nishi/Adi/Galo/Apatani), Bodo-Garo, Kuki-Chin-Naga (Meitei/Mizo/Ao/Angami/Sema/Konyak), Tibetic (Ladakhi/Bhutia/Sikkimese), Himalayish (Lepcha/Limbu)

#### 4. **Sumerian & Egyptian** (Cross-checked from Wikipedia)
| Language | Earliest Written | Estimated Age | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sumerian** | ~3,200 BCE | ~7,000–8,000 years | **Extinct** (~2,000 BCE spoken; ritual use till Hellenistic) |
| **Egyptian** | ~3,300 BCE | ~6,500–7,000 years | **Liturgical** (Coptic used in Coptic Church) |
| **Dravidian** | ~3rd c. BCE (Old Tamil) | ~6,000+ years | **Living** (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam) |

**Verdict**: Sumerian > Egyptian > Dravidian by attested written record. Proto-Dravidian may be contemporary with proto-Egyptian but no written evidence before 3rd c. BCE.

#### 6. **Indian Regional Language Mapping** (Cross-checked from Wikipedia)

**North India** (8 states + 2 UT — Northern Zonal Council):
- Punjabi, Hindi, Kashmiri, Dogri, Garhwali, Kumaoni, Himachali, Nepali, Urdu
- **Hindi belt** dominates; Punjabi unique cultural outlier

**South India** (5 states + 3 UT):
- **All 4 major = Dravidian + Classical**: Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam
- 27% Telugu, 27% Tamil, 17% Kannada, 13% Malayalam, 6% Urdu
- **Unique**: Entirely Dravidian bloc — no Indo-Aryan native languages

**Northeast India** (8 states):
- **~220 languages** across **4 families**: Indo-European (Bengali/Assamese/Nepali), Sino-Tibetan (Meitei, Bodo, Mizo, Naga languages), Austroasiatic (Khasi, Jaintia), Tai-Kadai (Ahom/Khamti)
- **Nagaland**: 20+ Naga languages → English official, Nagamese creole lingua franca
- **Arunachal**: 40-50 tribal languages → English official

**West India** (3 states + 1 UT):
- Marathi (73M), Gujarati (46M), Konkani (2.5M) — **all Indo-Aryan**
- Low diversity vs Northeast (1 family vs 4 families)

#### 7. **Age Ranking of Major Language Families** (Cross-checked)
1. **Sino-Tibetan** / **Austroasiatic** / **Austronesian** — ~6,000–9,000 years ago
2. **Indo-European** (proto) — ~5,500–6,000 years ago (Pontic Steppe)
3. **Dravidian** — ~4,000+ years ago (Indus Valley origin)
4. **Indo-Aryan in India** — ~3,500 years ago (migration)
5. **Tai-Kadai** — ~2,500–3,000 years ago (Southern China)
